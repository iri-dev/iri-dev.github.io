A letter about my name

Dear you,

My name is Iris Molina. For a long time I only went by my nickname online, but I'd like to be a bit more open with the people who use the things I make. If I'm asking for your trust, you deserve to know there's a real person here.

That said… please call me Iri! It's just Iris with the s chopped off, and I think it's cute. It's pronounced eye-ree, and you'll find me as iri-dev on GitHub.

I'm a software engineering and cyber security graduate, and I make the kind of software I always wished someone else would make. I want the things I build to help people feel safer, healthier, and more in control. This little room is where all of it lives; the rest of this page is me, in a bit more detail.

why I make things the way I do

The people behind the pixels ♡

There's something I care about more than the software I create: the people who use it.

I know that probably sounds like something every developer says, but I really do mean it.

When I'm building something, I don't just think about whether it works or how impressive the code is. I think about the person on the other side of the screen. Whether they'll understand it, whether it'll make their day a little easier, and whether they'll feel comfortable trusting something I've made.

I don't want my software to make people feel guilty, pressured, watched, or taken advantage of.

That's part of why I made HydraHeart. Taking care of yourself shouldn't feel like a competition or something you can fail at. And it's why I put so much effort into WardenOne. I believe people deserve to feel safer online without having to understand every technical detail of what's happening behind the scenes.

I also believe that helping people shouldn't always come with a price tag. I don't want to put basic features behind subscriptions, collect personal information I don't need, or make someone feel like they're missing out because they haven't paid me.

I'm not going to pretend I'm a perfect developer. I'll make mistakes, things will break, and there will always be something I could do better. But I want to listen when people tell me something isn't working, take their concerns seriously, and keep improving what I've made.

Even if something I build only helps one person, that's still one person whose day I might have made a little better.

And to me, that's worth making it. ♡

What you can hold me to

  • When something breaks

    Tell me. I read bug reports myself, I'll try to reply, and I'll fix what I can. If I can't fix something, I'll say so plainly instead of going quiet.

  • Your data stays yours

    I don't collect what I don't need. WardenOne has no account and no telemetry, and HydraHeart doesn't even ask for internet access. The simplest way to prove I don't want your data is to never collect any.

  • When I've got it wrong

    Say so. If you stop using something I made, I'd rather understand why than be told it's perfect. Getting better matters more to me than being right.

  • You stay in charge

    It's your device, so you get the final say. Every one of WardenOne's protections can be switched off, nothing basic sits behind a subscription, and every line of code is there to read.
